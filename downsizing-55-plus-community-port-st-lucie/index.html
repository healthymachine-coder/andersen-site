Senior Moving

Downsizing to a 55+ Community in Port St. Lucie

September 23, 2026 · 16 min read · Andersen Moving and Storage

Moving from a family home into a smaller villa is a big step. This guide walks Treasure Coast downsizers through a room-by-room plan, three popular local 55+ communities, Florida homestead portability, HOA gate rules, and how a mover fits in. It is written for local and in-state Florida movers, not long out-of-state hauls.

TLDR: Start with your new floor plan, not your old closets. Measure the rooms, keep what fits, and find new homes for the rest. Sort over months, not days. Protect your Save Our Homes tax benefit with portability. Then let a registered mover handle gates, HOA windows, and heavy lifting on moving day.

The Square-Footage Math Nobody Warns You About

A typical older Port St. Lucie home runs around 1,788 square feet. A Cascades Crystal-collection home can start near 1,257 square feet. The Four Seasons at Wylder villa, the Spring Lake IV model, starts at 1,572 square feet. That gap is real, and it is why downsizing feels so heavy at first.

Most people feel overwhelm and a little guilt. The dining set hosted every holiday. The garage holds decades of projects. Letting go of those things can feel like letting go of memories.

Here is the upside. Less square footage means less to clean and maintain. You trade yard work for a clubhouse, a pool, and pickleball. A lifestyle director plans the calendar. The trick is simple. Start with the new floor plan, not the old closets.

Moving Into a 55+ Community in Florida: Start With the New Floor Plan

Get the floor plan for your new home with room dimensions. Mark those dimensions on your current floors with painter’s tape. Now you can see what fits before you decide what to keep.

This one step prevents the most common downsizing mistake. People move too much, then live in a crowded home from day one. Clear floors also matter for safety. The National Institute on Aging lists clutter and loose rugs among the top fall hazards for older adults.

A Room-by-Room Downsizing Plan

Sorting room by room keeps a big job from feeling impossible. Finish one space before you start the next. Here is how we coach our senior downsizing clients across the Treasure Coast.

In the garage, tools sell better than china. Buyers want drills, ladders, and yard gear. Keep the tools you will still use in a smaller home. Sell or consign the rest.

In the dining room, take an heirloom approach. Keep one piece you love, like a hutch or a table. Pass the rest down to family who will use them. A formal set often lands beautifully with a grown child.

In the primary bedroom, measure before you move a king bed. Many villas fit a queen better. In guest rooms, cut down to one guest room or a den. You do not need three spare beds.

In the kitchen, keep one set of dishes plus a few entertaining basics. Donate the duplicates. For paperwork, secure your deed, insurance, birth certificates, and passports first. Then digitize old photos so the memories travel light.

On the lanai and in the yard, remember the HOA handles the lawn. That means the mower and edger can find a new home. Keep the patio furniture that fits your new lanai.

How Long Downsizing Really Takes

Downsizing usually takes 6 to 12 months. If you are also selling your home, plan for 12 to 18 months. Three months is the bare minimum, and it feels rushed.

Give yourself time so decisions stay calm and thoughtful. Rushed sorting leads to regret and higher stress. The table maps a comfortable timeline for a Treasure Coast downsizing move.

Don’t Leave Money on the Table: Homestead Portability

Homestead portability lets you move your Save Our Homes savings to your next Florida home. It can lower the taxable value of your new villa. Downsizers keep a percentage of the old benefit, not the whole dollar amount.

Here is a quick definition. Portability is the transfer of your Save Our Homes assessment difference from a prior Florida homestead to a new one, up to a $500,000 cap. You claim it with Form DR-501T, filed alongside your homestead application, by March 1.

When you downsize to a lower-value home, Florida uses the percentage rule. It divides your old assessed value by your old just value, then applies that percentage to the new home’s value. The example below is hypothetical and rounded for clarity.

Table 5. Portability Example (hypothetical)

StepOld HomeNew Villa
Just (market) value$500,000$350,000
Assessed value$300,000$210,000
Save Our Homes benefit$200,000 (40%)$140,000 transferred
Math40% of $500,00040% of $350,000

You must establish the new homestead by January 1 within three tax years of leaving the old one. This is not tax advice. Confirm with your county property appraiser. In St. Lucie County, call 772-462-1000. In Martin County, call 772-288-5608. See the St. Lucie County portability rules, the Martin County portability page, and the Florida Department of Revenue PT-112.

HOA and Gate Rules That Can Wreck Moving Day

Florida HOAs often restrict moves to weekday hours, such as 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Many block Sundays and holidays. Some require a certificate of insurance, a deposit, a truck-size limit, or a reservation.

Staffed gates add one more step. The community must have your mover on the guest list. A crew that shows up unlisted can get turned away at the gate.

This is exactly the kind of thing we handle. Our Port St. Lucie work already includes gate check-ins and HOA moving windows at communities like PGA Village, Tesoro, and The Vineyards. We ask for your gate procedure and move hours, then plan around them.

Request your HOA moving rules in writing early. Forward them to your mover. That single email prevents most move-day surprises.

FAQ

How far in advance should I start downsizing before moving to a 55+ community? Start 6 to 12 months out for a comfortable pace. If you are also selling your current home, plan for 12 to 18 months. Three months is the bare minimum. An early start keeps decisions calm and cuts down on last-minute stress.

How much of my stuff should I expect to get rid of? It varies by home and habits. At the high end, some downsizers part with as much as 80% of their belongings when moving to a much smaller space. That is not a rule, just the far end of the range. Start with your floor plan and keep what fits.

What furniture should I bring to a smaller villa? Bring the pieces that fit and that you use daily. Measure the bed first, since a king may crowd a villa bedroom. One sofa, a small dining set, and lanai furniture usually work well. Leave clear paths so the rooms stay safe and open.

Can I keep my Save Our Homes tax benefit when I downsize in Florida? Yes, through portability. When you downsize, Florida transfers the same percentage of your Save Our Homes benefit, up to a $500,000 cap. File Form DR-501T with your homestead application by March 1. Confirm the details with your county property appraiser.

Do 55+ communities in Port St. Lucie have moving-day rules? Most do. HOAs often limit moves to weekday hours and may block Sundays and holidays. Some require a certificate of insurance, a deposit, or a reservation. Staffed gates need your mover on the guest list, so send the rules to your crew early.

Should I hire a senior move manager or just a mover? It depends on the sort. A senior move manager plans and runs the decluttering and setup. A registered mover packs and moves. Many families use a move manager for the sort and a mover for the day. Others sort with family and just book the crew.

What if my new home isn’t ready when I have to leave my old one? Storage bridges the gap. We can hold your belongings between closings, then deliver when your new home is ready. Many downsizers store for a week or two while dates line up. We quote storage based on your volume and timeline.

Can movers set up my furniture and bed on moving day? Yes. We set up the bed and bathroom first so you have a place to rest. Then we assemble and place furniture to match your floor plan. You point, and we place. Please do not lift anything yourself.

Key Takeaways

  • Start with the floor plan. Measure the new rooms before you decide what to keep.
  • Give it time. Downsizing usually takes 6 to 12 months, longer if you are also selling.
  • Tools over china. Sell what buyers want and pass heirlooms down to family.
  • Protect your taxes. Portability moves a percentage of your Save Our Homes benefit; file by March 1.
  • Know the gate rules. HOA hours, COIs, and guest lists can stall a move if you skip them.
  • Set up bed and bathroom first. A calm first night matters more than a fully unpacked house.
