When to Book Your Snowbird Move to the Treasure Coast

August 20, 2026 · 10 min read · Andersen Moving and Storage

Snowbird arrivals on the Treasure Coast cluster between October and December, and moving crews fill those weeks first. If you plan to land in Florida this fall, August is the month the calendar still has room. This guide covers arrival windows, booking lead times, storage between seasons, and the condo rules that catch seasonal residents off guard.

TLDR: Fall snowbird arrivals fill moving calendars fast, and December runs tightest of all. Booking in August gets you your preferred date, more crew options, and time to clear condo or HOA paperwork. Wait until October and you compete with everyone else who waited. Read on for the arrival calendar and the booking deadline that matches your month.

You already know your target month. Maybe you close on a condo in November, or maybe you head south right after Thanksgiving like you do every year.

What most snowbirds miss is how early the good dates disappear. Movers do not fill evenly across the fall. They fill in a rush starting in September, as everyone books the same six or seven weeks.

This guide gives you the booking math, the storage decision, and the paperwork that stops moves at the gate.

Why August Is the Action Month for Snowbird Movers

August feels early. That is exactly why it works.

Crews still hold open weekdays across October and November. By late September those weeks look very different. Early bookers take the mid-week slots, and latecomers take what remains.

Booking early also steadies your rate, since movers price around demand.

The scale is real. Five of the ten counties with the most seasonal housing sit in Florida, according to U.S. Census Bureau seasonal housing analysis. Those snowbirds all arrive within a few weeks of each other.

Pro tip: Call before you finalize travel plans. A confirmed crew date makes a better anchor than a flight you can still change.

One detail saves real money. Mid-month and mid-week dates draw less demand than month-end, when leases and closings turn over.

Pro tip: Ask whether shifting your arrival two or three days opens a better slot. Small date changes often produce noticeable differences in availability.

What Happens If You Wait Too Long

Waiting rarely produces a disaster. It produces small compromises that add up.

Your date options shrink first. Last-minute pricing works against you next, since crews running a full calendar have little reason to discount.

The costliest part is losing choice. When one company has your date, you stop comparing and start accepting.

Verify any company before you sign. Florida requires household goods movers to register with FDACS, and legitimate companies share their registration number freely. Andersen Moving operates under Fla. Mover Reg. No. 3030. Read the state guidance on moving within Florida first.

Coming south across state lines? Federal rules apply, and the FMCSA guide to protecting your move explains how to check a carrier.

Illustrative scenario: A Michigan couple targets a November 15 arrival and books in mid-August, about ten weeks out. They secure the mid-week date they wanted before fall demand climbs.

Storage Solutions for the Months You Are Away

Snowbird living creates a question year-round residents never face. What comes with you, what stays, and where does the rest live?

Florida humidity drives that decision. Wood, artwork, electronics, and documents handle climate-controlled space better than a standard unit.

Pro tip: Photograph everything before it goes into storage. A dated photo set settles questions faster than memory does.

A Simple Action Plan to Book Your Fall Move Today

Five steps move you from intention to a confirmed date.

  1. Pick your arrival week, then count back 8 to 12 weeks.
  2. Request a written estimate separating labor, travel, materials, and access fees.
  3. Decide what goes into storage and what travels with you.
  4. Ask your association for move-in rules in writing.
  5. Confirm your date, then send that paperwork to your mover.

Steps four and five fail most often when snowbirds leave them to the final week.

Illustrative scenario: A family waits until late September to book a December arrival in a gated community. Their first-choice week is gone, so they shift to early January and lose six weeks of their season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When does snowbird season start in Florida?

A: Snowbird arrivals generally run October through April, with the heaviest wave landing between December and January. Ask your association when its own move-in traffic peaks.

Q: How far in advance should snowbirds book movers?

A: Plan on 8 to 10 weeks for most fall arrivals, and 10 to 12 for December. Late January and February often work with 6 to 8. Call as soon as you know your target week.

Q: Will it cost more to move during peak snowbird weeks?

A: Demand influences pricing, and the busiest snowbird weeks carry less flexibility on rates and dates. Booking early usually helps you avoid that pressure. Final costs still depend on volume, access, and services.

Q: Do I need storage if I only spend part of the year in Florida?

A: Many snowbirds do, especially for items that sit in a closed house all summer. Climate-controlled space suits wood furniture, artwork, and electronics. Standard storage works for patio furniture and tools.

Q: What if my condo or gated community has move-in restrictions?

A: Expect them, particularly in high-rise and 55-plus communities. Many require a certificate of insurance, an elevator reservation, and an approved move-in window. Request these rules two weeks out, then forward them to your mover.

Q: Is it better to arrive mid-month or at the end of the month?

A: Mid-month and mid-week dates usually see lower demand than month-end, when leases and closings cluster. That timing often gives you more crew options. If your closing date allows flexibility, use it.

Q: Can I book my move south and my move north at the same time?

A: You can plan both, and many snowbirds do. Raising the return trip on your first call helps us understand your storage needs. Confirm the northbound date closer to the season.

Q: What happens if I wait until October to book?

A: You will likely still find a mover, but your choices narrow. Preferred dates fill first, and last-minute availability rarely matches a specific week. The bigger risk is settling for a company you have not checked.

Key Takeaways

  • August is the booking month. Crews still hold open weekdays across October and November.

  • December runs tightest. Holiday snowbird arrivals need 10 to 12 weeks of lead time.

  • Your month sets your deadline. Count back 8 to 12 weeks and treat that date as real.

  • Mid-week beats month-end. Demand clusters around lease turnover, so flexible dates open options.

  • Association paperwork stops moves. Certificates, elevator reservations, and gate passes need two weeks.

  • Waiting costs choice. When one company has your date, you stop comparing.
