Why People Are Moving to Stuart in 2026

People moving to Stuart, Florida usually arrive for one of three reasons: taxes, schools, or the water. Stuart sits in Martin County, not St. Lucie County. That distinction matters more than most newcomers expect. Martin County runs its own school district, its own utilities, and its own development rules.

The financial case draws most people first. Florida charges no state income tax, and that exemption covers retirement income too. For a couple relocating from New York or Massachusetts, the annual difference often reaches four or five figures.

Property taxes carry a second protection. Once you homestead your primary residence, Florida’s Save Our Homes cap limits how fast your assessed value can climb each year. Long-term owners benefit the most from this rule.

The market itself has cooled from its frenzy years. Homes in Stuart now sit a median of 90 to 104 days before selling. You get time to think, inspect, and negotiate.

Pro tip: Ask your agent for the Martin County parcel number before you write an offer. You will need it later for utilities, insurance quotes, and your homestead application.

Handle utilities before closing day, not after. Cold showers and dark rooms make a first night miserable.

Martin County Utilities takes calls Monday through Friday, 8 AM to 5 PM. As of May 2026, the county no longer offers same-day new service after 2:30 PM, so call early in the day. The county utilities billing page lists current account details.

Florida law requires new residents to register vehicles within 10 days of starting a job, enrolling children in public school, or establishing residency. Put that task on your calendar before you leave your old state.

Frequently Asked Questions About Moving to Stuart

Q: What is the median home price in Stuart, Florida in 2026?

A: Estimates range from roughly $307,000 to $435,000 depending on the source and month measured. Most comprehensive 2026 figures land between $392,000 and $435,000. Median sale price read about $377,667 in May 2026. Neighborhood variation matters far more than the citywide number, so compare specific communities before you budget.

Q: What county is Stuart, Florida in?

A: Stuart serves as the county seat of Martin County. Port St. Lucie, just to the north, sits in St. Lucie County instead. That county line changes your school district, utility provider, and property tax rate. Confirm which county your address falls in before you set up services.

Q: Are Martin County schools good?

A: Martin County School District earned an A rating for the 2025-2026 school year, its second year in a row. It is the only A-rated district on the Treasure Coast. Every district-operated school earned a C or higher. Stuart Middle School and South Fork High School both improved from a B to an A.

Q: How much does it cost to live in Stuart compared to a high-tax state?

A: Florida charges no state income tax, including none on retirement income. Estimated monthly cost to own a typical Stuart home runs about $3,027 including taxes and insurance. Insurance premiums offset part of the tax savings, so run both numbers. For many relocating retirees and high earners, the math still favors Florida over time.

Q: What are the best neighborhoods in Stuart?

A: It depends on your budget and lifestyle. River Pines and Lexington Lakes offer the strongest value per square foot. Camellia balances price and pace for families. Willoughby Golf Club and Mariner Sands serve buyers wanting premium gated golf living, while Port Salerno suits boaters.

Q: Do I need to worry about HOA rules when moving into a Stuart community?

A: Yes, and this catches people off guard constantly. Many gated and 55-plus communities set specific moving windows and require a certificate of insurance from your mover. Some restrict weekend moves entirely. Request your community’s rules in writing at least two weeks before your move date.

Q: How do I set up utilities when moving to Stuart?

A: Call Florida Power & Light for electric service first. For water and sewer in an unincorporated area, contact Martin County Utilities at 772-221-1434. In-city addresses go through the City of Stuart utilities office instead. Start these calls about two weeks before closing, since county same-day service now ends at 2:30 PM.

Q: How long do I have to get Florida license plates after moving?

A: Florida law gives new residents 10 days. That clock starts when you begin employment, enroll children in public school, or establish residency. Visit the Martin County tax collector’s office with your title, proof of insurance, and identification. Schedule an appointment, since walk-in waits can run long.

Q: Is hurricane season a real problem for moving to Stuart?

A: Storm season runs June 1 through November 30, with peak activity from mid-August through mid-October. It affects timing, not whether you should move. Build flexibility into a summer moving date and start early in the day. Most summer moves on the Treasure Coast finish without any weather disruption.

Q: How far in advance should I book movers for a Stuart move?

A: Book at least two weeks ahead, and three to four weeks during peak season from October through April. Gated communities need that lead time for insurance paperwork anyway. We accommodate last-minute and emergency requests when our schedule allows. Call as soon as you have a closing date, even a tentative one.
