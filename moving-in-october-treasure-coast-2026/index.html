Moving in October on the Treasure Coast (2026): Open Dates, King Tides, and the Last Weeks of Hurricane Season

You have a move coming up in the next month or two. Maybe you heard fall is the cheaper season. Maybe you are a snowbird heading south, or you are trading a house for a condo. Either way, you want to know one thing: is October a good time to move here?

For most people, the answer is yes. The heat breaks, the summer crowd thins out, and you get your pick of weekday dates. Still, two October details catch people off guard, and neither shows up on a weather app.

The first is king tides, which can push salt water onto low streets on a sunny day. The second is the end of hurricane season, which stays open until November 30. This guide walks through both, plus costs, condo rules, and a week-by-week calendar.

Is October a Good Time to Move on the Treasure Coast?

Yes. October is one of the better months for moving in Florida. Summer demand has faded, mornings run cooler, and the big snowbird wave has not arrived yet. Hurricane season stays open through November 30, so the smart move is a flexible plan, not a different month.

October gives you more of what helps a move and less of what hurts one. There are two exceptions worth planning around. Here is how the month stacks up.

Moving in October: Costs and Availability

Many people ask, "Is October a cheap time to move?" Industry pricing guides list October through April as the lower-cost stretch for Florida moves. The bigger win in October is choice. You can usually pick a weekday morning instead of taking whatever slot is left.

Local movers price by crew size and hours, so home size drives your number more than distance. Stairs, long carries, and HOA time windows matter too. A long carry is the distance a crew walks from the truck to your door. Our local moving crews spell all of this out in a written quote.

King Tides: The October Moving Problem Nobody Mentions

King tides are the highest predicted tides of the year. They happen when the moon swings closest to Earth and lines up with the sun. On low streets near the water, they can push salt water onto the road with no rain at all. Martin County’s king tide page calls this sunny day flooding.

In Martin County, king tides usually hit in October and November. The 2026 king tide forecast from the South Florida Water Management District puts the East Coast peak around October 27. The district also warned that early October tides could reach minor flood levels.

Past high-tide flooding has shown up in Hobe Sound, at Dyer Point in Palm City, and in the St. Lucie Settlement area of Stuart. That does not mean those streets will flood on your date. It means you should check before you pick a start time.

Here are the 2026 king tide windows for Martin County. Use them to decide whether tides belong on your moving checklist.

Window Who Should Pay Attention What to Do
Sept 24 to Oct 15 Waterfront homes, riverfront condos Check the high-tide time before setting a start time
Oct 22 to Nov 12 (peak Oct 27) Barrier-island condos, low riverfront streets Avoid loading at high tide, keep a backup date
Nov 22 to Nov 29 Holiday-week movers near the water Plan around tide times and holiday traffic
Dec 22 to Dec 27 Snowbirds arriving for the holidays Confirm loading-zone access with your building

If you are moving to or from a waterfront street or a barrier-island condo, look up the high-tide time for that day. Then schedule loading for the hours before or after it.

Illustrative scenario: A couple planned to move from a rental into a two-bedroom riverfront condo downtown. Their first pick was October 27, the tide peak. They switched to a Thursday morning, reserved the elevator and loading zone, and sent the certificate of insurance a week ahead. A two-person crew finished in about five hours for roughly $900, with a dry loading area.

Pro tip: Look up the high-tide time for your street before you pick a start time. A two-hour shift can keep the truck out of standing water.

Moving During Hurricane Season in Florida: The Last Weeks

Moving during hurricane season in Florida worries a lot of people. The 2026 season has been unusually quiet, but it is still active. In early October, Tropical Storm Isaias formed in the Gulf, and forecasters expected it to become the season’s first hurricane. Its forecast track pointed at the northern Gulf Coast, far from the Treasure Coast.

Quiet seasons can still turn in October. On October 9, 2024, Hurricane Milton’s outer rainbands spun up a string of tornadoes across the region. The National Weather Service office in Melbourne documented tracks through Palm City, Port St. Lucie, and Stuart. Many local families still remember that afternoon.

A few simple steps keep storm season from derailing your plans:

  • Ask for a backup date in writing when you book.
  • Keep documents, medications, and chargers with you, not on the truck.
  • Check the National Hurricane Center five-day outlook the week of your move.
  • Follow your county emergency page if a watch or warning goes up.

Illustrative scenario: A family had a local move booked the same week a Gulf storm led the news. No watches or warnings covered St. Lucie County. They kept the date, confirmed a backup date in writing, and packed a go box with documents and medications. A three-person crew moved their three-bedroom home in about seven hours for roughly $1,500.

A storm somewhere in Florida does not mean your move is off. Watch the forecast for your county, not the whole state.

Treasure Coast Specifics: Condos, Gates, and HOA Move Windows

Many October movers are heading into a condo or a gated community. Those buildings and HOAs set their own rules, and they rarely bend on moving day.

  • Oceanfront buildings: Hutchinson Island buildings often require a certificate of insurance (COI) and a set move window. A COI is a document from your mover that proves they carry insurance.
  • Gate check-ins: Communities like Nettles Island and Ocean Breeze in Jensen Beach require crews to check in at the gate.
  • Riverfront condos: Stuart buildings often need elevator reservations and dock or loading-zone access.
  • Guard gates: Harbour Ridge and Martin Downs in Palm City run guard gates with set hours.
  • Port St. Lucie gated communities: PGA Village, Tesoro, and The Vineyards can limit which hours you can use.

Late-October snowbird arrivals in Jupiter and across Martin County compete for the same weekend gate slots. If your closing date and move date do not line up, short-term storage options can hold your things in between.

Illustrative scenario: A retiree downsized from a Palm City home into a smaller villa in Tradition on a mid-October Tuesday. The gated community allowed moves only from 8 AM to 4 PM. The crew checked in at the gate, finished in about six hours, and stayed inside the window. Total cost: about $1,200.

If you are weighing a similar step, our guide to downsizing into a 55-plus community covers the planning side.

Pro tip: Request your building’s move rules in writing two weeks out. Send them to your mover the same day you get them.

How to Hire a Mover in Florida Without Getting Burned

Florida has clear rules for movers who work inside the state. Every intrastate mover must register with FDACS, the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services. You can check any company through the state’s Business License Lookup or by calling 1-800-HELP-FLA. The FDACS guide to moving within Florida lays out your rights.

Under Chapter 507 of the Florida Statutes, movers must carry at least $10,000 in cargo liability per shipment. A mover who keeps your goods after you pay the written estimate or contract amount commits a third-degree felony. No mover may hold back prescription medicines or children’s items.

Before any work starts, your mover must give you a signed written estimate and contract. Here is what that document needs to include.

Item Why It Matters
Mover’s name, phone, address, and registration number Lets you verify the company with FDACS
Preparation date and move date Locks in your schedule
Pickup and delivery addresses Prevents confusion on moving day
Storage location, if any Tells you where your goods will sit
Itemized costs and services Shows exactly what you pay for
Accepted forms of payment Avoids surprises at delivery
Signatures from both parties Shows both sides agreed to the terms

If an estimate is missing any of these items, ask for a corrected one before you book. Our article on spotting moving scams in Florida covers the warning signs in more detail.

Your Action Plan for Moving in October

  1. Pick two possible dates, a first choice and a backup.
  2. Check the king tide windows and high-tide times for both addresses.
  3. Request your HOA or building move rules in writing.
  4. Get a written estimate and verify the mover’s FDACS registration.
  5. Book your crew, then send the COI request and gate details.
  6. Pack a go box with documents, medications, and chargers.
  7. Check the National Hurricane Center outlook the week of your move.
  8. Confirm your start time two days out, based on tides and HOA hours.

Frequently Asked Questions About Moving in October

Q: Is October a good time to move in Florida? A: Yes, for most people. October brings cooler mornings, more open dates, and less competition than summer or winter. The two catches are king tides near the water and the last weeks of hurricane season. Plan around both and October works well.

Q: Is it cheaper to move in October? A: Industry pricing guides list October through April as the lower-cost window for Florida moves. Your actual price depends on home size, crew, hours, and access. The clearest benefit in October is date choice. Ask for a written estimate and see whether a different date changes your quote.

Q: When does hurricane season end in Florida? A: The Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. In 2026, it ends on Monday, November 30, just after Thanksgiving. Peak activity usually runs from mid-August to mid-October. Late October and November tend to be quieter, but not storm-free.

Q: What happens if a storm is forecast for my moving day? A: Watch the National Hurricane Center and your county’s emergency page in the days before your move. If a watch or warning covers your area, call your mover right away to talk through options. That is why a backup date matters. Set it in writing when you book, not when the storm shows up.

Q: What are king tides, and will they affect my move? A: King tides are the highest predicted tides of the year. In 2026, Martin County lists windows from late September through late December, with an East Coast peak around October 27. They mostly affect low waterfront streets and barrier-island buildings. Check the high-tide time for your street and schedule loading around it.

Q: How far in advance should I book a mover for October? A: A couple of weeks usually works for a local weekday move. Book earlier for weekends, month-end dates, and late-October snowbird arrivals. Gated communities also need lead time for insurance paperwork. Call as soon as you have a closing or lease date.

Q: When do snowbirds arrive on the Treasure Coast? A: Arrivals start in late October and build through November. The biggest wave lands in December and January. Weekend dates fill first once that wave starts. If you are a snowbird, book your crew before November.

Q: Do movers work in the rain? A: Most crews keep working through ordinary Florida showers. They add floor protection and extra wrapping to keep your belongings dry. Lightning and severe weather are different, since crews pause outdoor work for safety. Ask your mover how they handle weather days before you book.

Q: Is it still too hot to move in October? A: October highs on the Treasure Coast usually sit in the low 80s. That is far easier than July or August, but early starts still help. Seniors and anyone sensitive to heat should plan for water breaks and shade. Our summer moving tips for seniors cover hydration and timing in more detail.

Q: How do I check that a Florida mover is legitimate? A: Look up the company through the FDACS Business License Lookup or call 1-800-HELP-FLA. A legitimate mover lists its registration number openly, like Andersen Moving’s Fla. Mover Reg. No. 3030. You should also get a signed written estimate before any work starts. Walk away from any company that refuses.

Key Takeaways

  • October is a strong month to move: Cooler mornings and more open dates make it easier than summer.
  • Check the tide calendar: King tides peak around October 27 and can flood low waterfront streets on dry days.
  • Hurricane season is not over: It runs through November 30, so set a backup date in writing.
  • Mid-month weekdays book easiest: October 12 to 21 is usually the smoothest stretch.
  • HOAs set the schedule: Request gate, COI, and move-window rules two weeks out.
  • Verify before you sign: Check FDACS registration and get a written estimate with every required item.
